Phase 1 — Started with AI

First I started with AI. My own sessions, my own questions — the AI executed, I directed. It began with one question: "what's the biggest problem?" The answer was the strange repeating glyphs — and I said, if these repeat, those are clues. That's footprints. The method, the glyph files, the breakthrough: that's all Phase 1, before anybody else was in the picture.

Part A — The method

Shannon's DeepSeek thread, Feb 15 2026, ~11:20–11:27 AM
He didn't start by guessing what the language was. He started with a question nobody else asked: how often do the glyphs repeat? The answer — constantly, in patterns — became a 7-step codebreaking method. "Not by guessing what the language is, but by letting the manuscript teach you its own grammar."

Part B — The glyph files

Shannon's DeepSeek thread, Feb 15 2026, ~11:27 AM–11:44 AM
Pick the most frequent glyph. Follow it page to page. Write down everything. That's the "qo file" — and then the "dy file." Shannon's rule the whole way: "If it fails, you adjust."

Part C — The breakthrough

Shannon's DeepSeek thread, Feb 15 2026, ~11:54 AM–3:01 PM
Then it tipped over. The glyphs aren't the text — they're the map to the text. "600-year-old hyperlinks." The book isn't a manuscript, it's a manuelscript — built to be used, not just read. And Shannon named the mechanism himself: "a paragraph with a 1 at the end of it."
